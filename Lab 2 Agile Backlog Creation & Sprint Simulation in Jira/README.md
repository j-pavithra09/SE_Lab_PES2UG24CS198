# Telemedicine Portal — Sprint Planning & Tracking

**Course/Lab:** Software Engineering Lab 2  
**Project:** Telemedicine Portal

## Overview

This lab documents Agile backlog planning and simulated sprint tracking for a Telemedicine Portal. The backlog covers doctor discovery, specialty filtering, consultation-slot booking, consultation completion, secure room-link handling, booking notifications, and prescription delivery.

The deliverables include user stories tracked on a sprint board, story-point estimates, sprint progress, a burndown chart, and reflective answers about estimation, prioritization, and team capacity.

## Sprint Planning

The report describes a simulated sprint plan with foundational patient-facing booking and slot-selection features prioritized first, followed by security-critical room-link and prescription features.

- **Sprint 1:** Focus on doctor discovery, specialty filtering, available-slot viewing, and slot booking.
- **Sprint 2:** Focus on consultation completion, room-link handling, booking notifications, and prescription writing/signing and download.

The screenshots show sprint-board and backlog views used to track work items and their status.

## Progress Tracking

The lab includes a burndown chart showing remaining story points declining over the sprint timeline. The reflection states that progress was steady, there were no blockers in the simulated workflow, and the planned sprints were completed within the stated one-week duration.

## Reflection

### 1. Did the estimates reflect the actual effort?

The report says estimates generally reflected the effort: simpler tasks such as viewing doctors were quicker, while more complex tasks such as encrypted video-link generation required more steps and time to simulate.

### 2. Was the backlog well-prioritized?

The backlog prioritized foundational patient-facing features such as booking and slot selection before security-critical and prescription features.

### 3. How did the simulated sprint align with the plan?

According to the report, stories progressed through **To Do → In Progress → Done** in priority order without blockers, and both sprints were completed within the planned one-week duration.

### 4. What did the burndown chart indicate about team capacity?

The report describes a steady decline in remaining story points and estimates team capacity at approximately **18–19 story points per sprint**, suggesting that the estimates were realistic.
